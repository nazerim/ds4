The single governing factor is the **geological structure of the catchment**, specifically the presence of **fractured bedrock**. This factor dictates the hydrological pathway because it shifts the primary source of baseflow from shallow soil moisture to deep groundwater storage within rock fractures. Since water movement through fractured bedrock is governed by the permeability and connectivity of the rock matrix rather than the rapid saturation of surface soils, the release of water into the stream is significantly slower and more buffered. Consequently, this geological characteristic decouples the stream's response from immediate rainfall intensity, resulting in a sustained, lagged flow that is insensitive to short-term precipitation spikes.
